# EVIDENCE DOSSIER — "Plastic Protocols"
**Single source of truth for site content. Last graded: 2026-06-03.**

**Grade key:** `strong` = robust, multi-source, often regulatory consensus · `emerging` = real signal, real sources, but contested/unreplicated/uncertain magnitude · `weak` = thin or single-source, or directionally true but headline numbers overstated · `unproven` = no supporting human evidence for the specific claim · `debunked` = popular figure shown to be wrong.

**Cross-cutting rule for the whole site:** Separate *plastic PARTICLES* (microplastics/nanoplastics — exposure proven, harm largely unproven in humans) from *associated CHEMICALS* (BPA, phthalates, PFAS — better-evidenced toxicology, regulatory action). Never merge them into one "plastics are toxic" message.

## (a) What microplastics are & how much we're really exposed to

### Two dominant routes: ingestion + inhalation; skin is negligible for intact particles — `emerging`
Ingestion and inhalation are the two dominant exposure routes, of broadly comparable magnitude in the most-cited estimate; intact microplastics (>1 µm) do not cross intact skin (only sub-100 nm nanoplastics show limited penetration, mainly through damaged skin). Absolute numbers carry large uncertainty and "comparable magnitude" is genuinely debated (some estimates put inhalation far higher). Note: skin absorption of *chemicals* (BPA, phthalates) is a separate, more permeable question.
- Cox et al., *Human Consumption of Microplastics*, Environ. Sci. Technol. (2019) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.9b01517
- *Penetration of Microplastics and Nanoparticles Through Skin*, PMC (2025) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11755607/

### Most-cited intake estimate: ~74,000–121,000 particles/year — `emerging`
~39,000–52,000 particles/year from food, rising to ~74,000–121,000 with inhalation; bottled-water drinkers ingest ~90,000/year extra vs ~4,000 for tap. These are PARTICLE COUNTS (not mass), cover only ~15% of US caloric intake, and likely undercount. A 76-study review found per-day estimates spanning ~0.0002 to ~1.5 million — method heterogeneity makes any single number unreliable.
- Cox et al. (2019) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.9b01517
- Heo, Moon & Kim, systematic review, Rev. Environ. Health (2024) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39431565/

### All intake/tissue numbers carry order-of-magnitude uncertainty — `strong`
Microplastic measurement is not standardized: lab gloves/dust shed polyethylene-like particles inflating counts; particle-counting vs mass-based pyrolysis-GC/MS produce non-comparable datasets; no agreed blank protocol exists. ISO methods are still in development; full EU drinking-water risk assessment is due 2029. Treat every specific number as an order-of-magnitude indication.
- *Do we really have enough plastic in our brains to make a spoon?*, C&EN (2026) — https://cen.acs.org/analytical-chemistry/diagnostics/really-enough-microplastics-brains-make-spoon/104/web/2026/04
- Heo, Moon & Kim (2024) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39431565/

### Authorities have NOT established harm at current exposure levels — `strong`
WHO's 2019 drinking-water report and 2022 micro/nanoplastics review concluded available evidence does not demonstrate a health risk at current levels, while flagging major data gaps (especially for the smallest nanoplastics). This is a statement about the evidence base, not a clean bill of health. *This is essential framing for the whole site.*
- WHO, *Microplastics in drinking-water: Key questions and answers* (2019/2022) — https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/microplastics-in-dw-information-sheet190822.pdf
- WHO, *Dietary and inhalation exposure to nano- and microplastic particles* (2022) — https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240054608

## (b) Sources in daily life, ranked by contribution

**Confidence ranking for human exposure (highest-confidence + highest-impact first):** bottled water → heated/microwaved plastic containers → indoor air/dust (plastic fraction only) → cookware (scratched non-stick) → seafood/salt (minor). Cosmetic microbeads are effectively a non-issue post-ban.

### Bottled water — one of the largest measured particle sources — `strong`
2024 PNAS study (stimulated Raman scattering) found ~240,000 plastic particles/L (range 110,000–370,000), ~90% nanoplastics — 10–100× older estimates, because the method detects down to 100 nm. This is a *detection advance*, not necessarily rising contamination. Bottled holds ~3× more nanoplastic than treated tap (separate Ohio State study). High EXPOSURE, not proven harm.
- Qian et al., *Rapid single-particle chemical imaging of nanoplastics by SRS microscopy*, PNAS (2024) — https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2300582121
- *Some bottled water worse than tap for microplastics*, Ohio State — https://news.osu.edu/some-bottled-water-worse-than-tap-for-microplastics-study-shows/

### Heating/microwaving plastic containers — high-intensity, controllable — `emerging`
Microwaving certain FDA-approved PP/PE containers released up to ~4.2 million microplastic + ~2.1 billion nanoplastic particles per cm² within 3 min — far more than fridge/room-temp storage. These are lab measurements into food simulants (per cm², not daily intake); the cytotoxicity finding was in-vitro at doses ~5–6 orders of magnitude above realistic exposure. A published critique argues the driver is temperature, not the microwave method per se — but the directional result (heat sharply increases release) holds.
- Hussain et al., Environ. Sci. Technol. (2023) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.3c01942
- Univ. of Nebraska-Lincoln / EurekAlert (2023) — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/996162

### Indoor air & household dust — real route, but plastic fraction overstated — `weak`
Indoor fiber counts exceed outdoor and synthetic textiles/carpets/furnishings are the dominant *plastic* source. BUT a large fraction of indoor "microfibers" are natural (cotton, wool) — labeling the whole 0.4–59 fibers/m³ range as "microplastic" overstates the plastic share by ~10×. Lung-deposition figures come from modeling, not direct human measurement.
- Dris et al., *Textile fibers in indoor and outdoor environments*, Environmental Pollution (2017) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27989388/
- Liu et al., *Atmospheric microfibers dominated by natural and regenerated cellulosic fibers* (2022) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0269749122019856

### Seafood (bivalves) & table salt — real but minor — `emerging`
Bivalves carry the highest seafood concentrations because they're eaten whole including the gut; gutted finfish fillets contribute little. Salt is consistently minimal in total-diet estimates. Reported concentrations span orders of magnitude. (The often-quoted "up to ~70 particles/g" bivalve upper bound is overstated vs documented maxima ~35–44/g.)
- Cox et al. (2019) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.9b01517
- *Concentration of microplastics in bivalves: a systematic review*, Environ. Monit. Assess. (2021) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10661-021-09639-1

### Cosmetic microbeads — no longer a meaningful source — `strong`
Banned by the US Microbead-Free Waters Act of 2015 (rinse-off manufacturing stopped July 2017; toothpaste July 2018), with parallel EU/UK/Canada bans; even pre-ban they were ~0.03–1.5% of marine plastic debris. The ban covers only rinse-off products, not leave-on cosmetics. *Do not prioritize cosmetics as a particle-exposure concern.*
- *Microbead-Free Waters Act of 2015: FAQs*, FDA — https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/microbead-free-waters-act-faqs
- H.R.1321, Public Law 114-114, Congress.gov — https://www.congress.gov/bill/114th-congress/house-bill/1321

## (c) Health effects — PARTICLES

### Particles detected across human tissues — `strong` (detection only)
Micro/nanoplastics physically detected in blood, placenta, lungs, liver, kidney, testes/semen, atherosclerotic plaque, and brain — detection across tissues is reproducible across methods. Caveats: serious procedural-contamination risk inflates *absolute concentrations* (which remain contested); detection ≠ biological effect. The "spoonful (~7 g) of plastic in the brain" figure is a contested extrapolation, not a weighed mass.
- Nihart et al., *Bioaccumulation of microplastics in decedent human brains*, Nature Medicine (2025) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03453-1
- Leslie et al., *Plastic particle pollution in human blood*, Environment International (2022) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412022001258

### Cardiovascular signal: NEJM carotid-plaque study — `emerging` (strongest human signal)
Marfella et al. (2024, n=304/257, ~34 mo follow-up): patients with micro/nanoplastics in carotid plaque had a ~4.5× higher risk of heart attack, stroke, or death (HR 4.53, 95% CI 2.00–10.27). This is the highest-quality prospective human OUTCOME data in the field — and why microplastics are taken seriously. BUT it is observational (association, not causation), single-center, unreplicated, with contamination concerns and an unusually large HR. Cite as a landmark hypothesis-generating signal, not established causation.
- Marfella et al., NEJM 2024;390:900-910 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38446676/
- ACC Journal Scan (confirms figures + causation caveat) — https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2024/03/07/19/21/microplastics-and-nanoplastics

### Mechanism: particles trigger oxidative stress / inflammation in cells & animals — `emerging`
In cell-culture and animal models, plastic particles induce oxidative stress and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) and are recognized by macrophages/dendritic cells. Caveats: most studies use pristine polystyrene spheres at doses far above realistic exposure; the "independent of leached chemicals" framing is overstated (co-contaminants/endotoxin can confound); human relevance at real-world doses is not established.
- *Molecular and Cellular Effects of Microplastics and Nanoplastics*, Cells (2024) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11545702/
- *Potential Artifacts and Control Experiments in Toxicity Tests*, Environ. Sci. Technol. (2023) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10476161/

### 2025 mouse study: microplastics promote arterial plaque — `emerging`
In male (not female) LDLR-deficient mice on a lean diet, environmentally-relevant microplastic doses promoted arterial plaque — controlled animal evidence supporting the cardiovascular hypothesis. Caveats: authors state it does not prove causation; sex-specific; the model is genetically atherosclerosis-prone (microplastics accelerate, not initiate); one headline endpoint was borderline-significant (p=0.054); not the "first" such study.
- Lin et al., *Microplastic exposure elicits sex-specific atherosclerosis...*, Environment International (2025) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412025006890
- UC Riverside News — https://news.ucr.edu/articles/2025/11/18/microplastics-hit-male-arteries-hard

### Testes/semen: correlation with sperm-quality markers — `emerging`
Particles detected in 100% of human/canine testes examined; some studies correlate plastics with lower sperm count/quality. But the sperm-count correlation was strongest in dogs; human data are cross-sectional/limited; confounding is likely. Distinct from endocrine-disrupting chemicals (phthalates/BPA), which have a stronger reproductive-toxicity literature.
- *UNM Researchers Find Microplastics in Canine and Human Testicular Tissue* (2024) — https://hscnews.unm.edu/news/hsc-newsroom-post-microplastics-testicular
- *Association of microplastics with sperm dysfunction: a multi-site study in China*, eBioMedicine (2024) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11663775/

### Overall: no conclusive evidence particles CAUSE any specific human disease — `weak` (i.e., evidence of harm is limited)
The honest overall state of the science as of 2025: the human evidence base is dominated by detection and correlation, not demonstrated causal harm. "Weak" means limited/indirect human evidence of harm — NOT that plastics are proven safe. The precautionary case rests on plausibility plus rising exposure.
- *Impact of Microplastic Exposure on Human Health: A Systematic Review*, Cureus (2025) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12848325/
- *Microplastics: State of the Evidence on Health Effects*, Dtsch. Arztebl. Int. (2025) — https://di.aerzteblatt.de/int/archive/article/246672

## (d) Health effects — associated CHEMICALS (BPA / phthalates / PFAS)

*Headline for this section: the harm from plastics is better-evidenced for these chemicals than for the particles themselves.* — `emerging` (comparative claim)
The "Trojan horse" hypothesis (particles carrying adsorbed pollutants/additives into the body) is plausible and environmentally demonstrated but unquantified in humans. Prioritize reducing chemical exposure over chasing particle "detox."
- Symeonides et al., *Umbrella Review of Meta-Analyses... Plastic-Associated Chemicals*, Annals of Global Health (2024) — https://annalsofglobalhealth.org/articles/10.5334/aogh.4459

### EFSA cut the BPA tolerable daily intake ~20,000-fold; EU banned BPA in food contact 20 Jan 2025 — `strong`
April 2023: EFSA cut the TDI ~20,000× to 0.2 ng/kg bw/day, citing immune (Th17) effects; EU Regulation 2024/3190 bans BPA (and BPS/BPAF) in food-contact materials. This is a *regulatory-action* fact, not a clinical-harm fact: the critical endpoint is an intermediate immune marker in mice, and the magnitude is contested. Note "BPA-free" substitutes (BPS, BPF) are also endocrine-active.
- EFSA Journal 2023;21(4):6857 — https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2023.6857
- EU Regulation 2024/3190 (Access2Markets) — https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/eu-prohibition-use-and-trade-bisphenol-20-january-2025

### EFSA's BPA reassessment is disputed by EMA and Germany's BfR — `strong`
EMA and BfR rejected the new TDI, arguing the Th17 endpoint is not a demonstrated adverse health outcome; BfR derived its own TDI ~1,000× higher. The direction (less BPA) is consensus; the *magnitude* of risk is where regulators genuinely disagree. Don't read "cut 20,000×" as "BPA proven 20,000× more dangerous."
- *Report on divergent opinion between EFSA and EMA on bisphenol A* (EMA/150385/2023) — https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-divergent-opinion-between-efsa-and-ema-bisphenol_en.pdf
- *Evidence evaluated by EFSA does not support lowering the tTDI for BPA*, Toxicol. Sci. (2024) — https://academic.oup.com/toxsci/article/198/2/185/7587524

### Phthalates (DEHP) are anti-androgenic; human reproductive link is supportive but mixed — `emerging`
Robust anti-androgenic "phthalate syndrome" in rodents; human epidemiology is supportive but observational and inconsistent across metabolites. Important controversy: human fetal-testis explant/xenograft studies show the human fetal testis is relatively insensitive to phthalate testosterone suppression ("man is not a big rat"). About the CHEMICAL, not particles.
- Radke et al. (U.S. EPA), *Phthalate exposure and male reproductive outcomes: systematic review* (2018) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30336412/
- Habert et al., *Man is not a big rat* (2014) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4349750/

### PFAS: strongest human evidence; PFOA now IARC Group 1 — `strong`
PFAS linked to raised cholesterol, reduced vaccine antibody response, and kidney cancer (NASEM 2022 strongest tiers). IARC (Nov 2023) classified PFOA Group 1 (carcinogenic to humans), PFOS Group 2B. US EPA finalized 4 ppt drinking-water limits for PFOA/PFOS (April 2024). Caveat: Group 1 is a HAZARD identification (rested largely on animal + mechanistic data; direct human cancer evidence "limited"), not a measured risk at typical exposures. PFAS are coating-associated industrial chemicals, not particles or plasticizers.
- *IARC Monographs evaluate carcinogenicity of PFOA and PFOS* (Vol 135, 2023) — https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/iarc-monographs-evaluate-the-carcinogenicity-of-perfluorooctanoic-acid-pfoa-and-perfluorooctanesulfonic-acid-pfos/
- *Guidance on PFAS Exposure, Testing, and Clinical Follow-Up*, NASEM (2022) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK584690/

### BPA & metabolic harm: emerging experimental human signal — `emerging`
A 2024 double-blind RCT (n=40) found BPA at the US EPA reference dose reduced peripheral insulin sensitivity within ~4 days — a real causal signal, but tiny, short-term, on a surrogate marker (not diabetes outcomes). Supports observational BPA–type-2-diabetes links. (Attributable-burden figures like "127 million metabolic-disease cases" are causality-assuming modeling — illustrative, not measured.)
- Hagobian et al., *Bisphenol A decreases peripheral insulin sensitivity in normal weight adults: RCT*, J. Clin. Endocrinol. Metab. — https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag124
- *Exposure to BPA and BPS and Incident Type 2 Diabetes (D.E.S.I.R.)*, Environ. Health Perspect. (2019) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6867193/

## (e) Environmental effects & wastewater pathway

### Wastewater plants remove most microplastics, but shift them to sludge → farmland — `strong` (with "well over 95%" overstated)
Modern plants remove a large majority of influent microplastics (realistic central tendency ~88–94%; only best-in-class membrane bioreactors reach >99%). Captured particles concentrate in sewage sludge; land-applying biosolids returns them to soils (documented to persist >22 years). The "well over 95%" framing cherry-picks the optimistic tail; huge water volumes mean residual percentages still release large absolute numbers daily.
- *Efficiency of WWTPs for Microplastic Removal: A Systematic Review*, IJERPH (2020) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7663475/
- *Microplastics in agricultural soils following sewage sludge applications: 25-year study*, Sci. Total Environ. (2025) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S004565352500219X

### Washing-machine microfibre capture devices cut emissions to waterways (efficiency varies hugely) — `strong`
External filters (XFiltra ~78%, Lint LUV-R ~80–87%) >> Guppyfriend bag (~54%) >> Cora Ball (~26% by count, ~5% by weight). CRITICAL for a health site: these reduce ENVIRONMENTAL emissions, NOT the user's personal inhalation/ingestion exposure.
- Napper, Barrett & Thompson, *Efficiency of devices to reduce microfibre release during clothes washing*, Sci. Total Environ. (2020) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048969720339346
- McIlwraith et al., Marine Pollution Bulletin (2019) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30686443/

## (f) What you can do — reduction protocols (evidence-graded)

*All particle-reduction swaps are precautions: exposure reduction is measurable; downstream health benefit is unproven (WHO/FDA/EFSA). Chemical-reduction swaps (canned/fresh) have stronger biomarker evidence.*

### Tap over bottled water — `strong` (exposure reduction)
Bottled water typically holds far more particles than tap; switching cuts a large, quantifiable share of dietary intake. ("Single best-supported way" is a fair but unranked superlative; a UK study found tap and bottled indistinguishable, so it's not universal.)
- Qian et al., PNAS (2024) — https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2300582121

### Fresh/unpackaged over canned food (lowers BPA + DEHP biomarkers) — `strong` (chemical, human intervention)
Human intervention trials: 3 days of fresh food cut urinary BPA ~66% and DEHP metabolites ~53–56% (Rudel 2011); one can of soup/day raised urinary BPA ~1,221% vs fresh (Carwile 2011). Measures CHEMICAL biomarkers, not particles; magnitude depends on packaging vintage.
- Rudel et al., *Food Packaging and BPA/DEHP Exposure: A Dietary Intervention*, Environ. Health Perspect. (2011) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3222978/
- Carwile et al., *Canned Soup Consumption and Urinary BPA: RCT* (2011) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3367259/

### Heat/store food in glass or ceramic, never microwave plastic — `emerging` (particle) / `strong` (chemical migration)
Heating plastic is the highest-release scenario for particles; glass/ceramic controls show minimal background. Heat also drives migration of BPA/phthalate chemicals. Low-cost, dual-benefit.
- Hussain et al., Environ. Sci. Technol. (2023) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.3c01942

### Metal/cast-iron cookware; discard scratched non-stick — `strong`
Direct controlled comparison (Cole 2024): stainless/glass cookware added no microplastics above background; plastic/PTFE released significantly more. Scratched Teflon can shed thousands–millions of particles (Luo 2022, extrapolated). PTFE is a PFAS-family fluoropolymer.
- Cole et al., *Microplastic and PTFE contamination of food from cookware*, Sci. Total Environ. (2024) — https://plymsea.ac.uk/10199/1/Cole%20et%20al%20(2024)%20STOTEN.pdf
- Luo et al., Sci. Total Environ. (2022) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S004896972205392X

### Loose-leaf tea / plastic-free bags over nylon-PET "silken" tea bags — `weak` (overstated)
Plastic mesh bags shed more under hot extraction than alternatives, but the famous "billions per cup" figure is methodologically contested (BfR: 2–3 orders of magnitude too high; mostly oligomers/artifacts). Many "paper" bags also contain plastic sealant. Sensible, cheap swap; don't cite the billions figure as fact. *(See Myths.)*
- Hernandez et al. (2019) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31552738/
- BfR assessment 029/2025 — https://www.bfr.bund.de/en/notification/bfr-assesses-study-on-tea-bags-and-microplastic-particles/

### Boil + filter hard water; use a membrane/RO filter — `emerging`
In HARD water, boiling then filtering out limescale removed ~80–90% of particles; in soft water only ~25% (single lab study, spiked water, debated). Reverse-osmosis/sub-micron membranes remove the large majority; basic carbon-only pitcher filters are unreliable and can shed particles.
- Yu et al., *Drinking Boiled Tap Water Reduces Human Intake of Nano/Microplastics*, Environ. Sci. Technol. Lett. (2024) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.estlett.4c00081
- Cherian et al., *Microplastic Removal Using Point-of-Use Devices*, Polymers (2023) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10054062/

### Use a glass/steel kettle; discard first boils of a new plastic kettle — `emerging`
Polypropylene kettles shed millions of nanoparticles/L on first boils, rising with temperature, falling >96% after ~150 cycles. Particle counts, not health effects.
- Shi, Okoffo, Rauert, Thomas et al., *Release of nanoplastic from polypropylene kettles*, npj Emerging Contaminants (2025) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s44454-025-00018-w

### Replace black recycled-plastic utensils (flame-retardant chemicals) — `weak`
Black plastic from recycled e-waste can contain banned brominated flame retardants (decaBDE). CHEMICAL-contamination, not particles. The original "near the safety limit" framing was wrong (twice-corrected; corrected exposure <1% of the EPA reference dose); presence stands. Cheap, harmless to swap.
- Liu et al. Corrigendum, Chemosphere (2024) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S004565352402811X
- *Viral paper earns second correction*, Retraction Watch (2025) — https://retractionwatch.com/2025/07/06/viral-paper-black-plastic-kitchen-spatula-utensils-chemosphere-second-correction/

### Wood/bamboo cutting boards instead of plastic — `weak` (overstated)
Plastic boards shed tens of grams/year of microplastic. BUT correction: wood boards shed 4–22× MORE total particles (just not plastic), and a 2025 in-vivo mouse study found cutting-board microplastics caused gut inflammation — so "no toxicity" no longer holds. Reasonable swap to avoid plastic specifically; frame honestly.
- Yadav et al., *Cutting Boards: An Overlooked Source of Microplastics?*, Environ. Sci. Technol. (2023) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.est.3c00924

### Indoor air: ventilate + HEPA vacuum + reduce synthetic-textile load — `emerging` (ventilation, dust) / `weak` (HEPA purifiers)
Ventilation lowers indoor airborne microplastics when outdoor air is cleaner (can backfire in polluted areas). Greater vacuuming frequency correlated with lower dust microplastic loading; sealed HEPA vacuums avoid re-emitting fines; shoe removal is extrapolated from tracked-in-contaminant literature. HEPA *purifiers*: mechanistically plausible and cut general PM2.5, but microplastic-specific removal is largely manufacturer-claimed and partly contradicted (air agitation can re-suspend fibres) — `weak`.
- Choi et al., *Microplastics indoor vs outdoor air and ventilation rate effect (Seoul)*, Emerging Contaminants (2024) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405665024001094
- Catarino et al., *International quantification of microplastics in indoor dust* (2022) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35977640/

### Natural-fibre clothing (with caveats) — `strong`
Switching from synthetics reduces specifically PLASTIC fibre shedding, but cotton/wool shed comparable or greater fibre MASS; fabric construction matters as much as fibre type. Natural fibres' real advantage is biodegradability + no plastic additives. Wash less, cooler, gentler.
- Stanton et al., *Freshwater and airborne fibres dominated by 'natural', not microplastic, fibres* (2019) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048969719307764
- Vassilenko et al., *Domestic laundry and microfiber pollution*, PLOS One (2021) — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0250346

## (g) Myths & overstated claims to correct

### "We eat ~5 g (a credit card) of plastic per week" — `debunked`
Traces to a 2019 WWF/Newcastle estimate that was the extreme upper bound (0.1–5 g range) of a few food sources, conflated with a 2019 inhalation study that estimated no mass. Recalculations put realistic ingestion orders of magnitude lower (~micrograms/week). Lead author called the credit-card line "a bit like a joke." **Do not use this figure.**
- *You do not inhale a credit card's worth of microplastic every week*, Full Fact (2024) — https://fullfact.org/health/credit-card-microplastic-week/
- Mohamed Nor et al., *Lifetime Accumulation of Microplastic*, Environ. Sci. Technol. (2021) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.0c07384

### "There's a spoonful (~5,000 µg/g, ~0.5% by weight) of plastic in the brain" — `emerging`/overstated
The 2025 Nature Medicine brain study's *directional* findings (plastics present, rising over time, brain > liver/kidney) are striking and partly corroborated. But the absolute mass / "spoon" figure is contested: pyrolysis-GC/MS misreads lipid fragments as polyethylene, reported brain levels implausibly exceed sewage sludge, and supplementary images were duplicated (corrected). Present as a contested early signal; avoid the "spoon" framing.
- Nihart et al., Nature Medicine (2025) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03453-1
- *Do we really have enough plastic in our brains to make a spoon?*, C&EN (2026) — https://cen.acs.org/analytical-chemistry/diagnostics/really-enough-microplastics-brains-make-spoon/104/web/2026/04

### "Plastic tea bags release billions of particles per cup" — `weak`/contested
Real that plastic bags shed more than alternatives, but the "11.6 billion + 3.1 billion per cup" figure is 2–3 orders of magnitude too high per a published Comment and Germany's BfR (mostly dissolved oligomers, a drying artifact); BfR found no health risk at reported levels.
- Hernandez et al. (2019) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31552738/
- BfR 029/2025 — https://www.bfr.bund.de/en/notification/bfr-assesses-study-on-tea-bags-and-microplastic-particles/

### "Donating blood/plasma removes microplastics from your body" — `unproven`
No human study shows routine blood/plasma DONATION lowers microplastic-PARTICLE levels. The cited evidence (firefighter RCT) measured PFAS *chemicals*, not particles — a category error. Donation can't reach tissue-sequestered particles. (Donate blood to help others, not to "detox.")
- *Effect of Plasma and Blood Donations on PFAS in Firefighters: RCT*, JAMA Network Open (2022) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8994130/

### "Saunas / sweating detox microplastics" — `debunked`
No evidence particles exit in sweat (they're too large); only some leached *chemicals* appear in sweat (small, old, weak studies). A 2024 study found sweat causes plastic additives to leach INTO skin — the opposite of detox. Enjoy saunas for relaxation, not microplastic removal.
- Genuis et al., *Blood, Urine, and Sweat (BUS) Study* (2011) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21057782/
- *Toxic chemicals from microplastics can be absorbed through skin*, Univ. of Birmingham (2024) — https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/04/240419131901.htm

### "Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) detoxes microplastics" — `weak`/commercial
One uncontrolled, industry-funded study (company's own clinics, proprietary unvalidated assay) reported circulating microplastics fell ~60% after TPE. No randomization, no health outcomes, ~$5–8k/session, and the tubing can add particles. Do not pay for "microplastic detox."
- Weinstein et al., *Can Plasma Exchange Lower the Circulating Burden of Microplastics?*, J. Clinical Apheresis (2026) — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jca.70135
- *Company verifies first microplastics detox procedure. Results vary.*, E&E News/POLITICO (2026) — https://www.eenews.net/articles/company-verifies-first-microplastics-detox-procedure-results-vary/

### "Antioxidant / mitochondrial supplements remove or detox microplastics" — `unproven`
No human trials; no antioxidant REMOVES particles. At best, in cells/rodents (at unrealistic doses) they blunt oxidative stress. High-dose antioxidants can turn pro-oxidant. Save your money.
- *Antioxidant Intervention Against Microplastic Hazards* (review, 2025) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12291741/

### "Fibre / raw vegetables flush microplastics out" — `weak`
Plausible mechanism, no human evidence for the foods claimed (only an off-target chitosan-supplement signal). A viral "UCLA, 57% reduction in urinary biomarkers" claim is **fabricated** — do not cite it. Eat fibre for established reasons, not microplastic removal.
- Wang et al., *Dietary fibers... protecting health*, Food Frontiers (2024) — https://iadns.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fft2.437
- *Does fibre really flush microplastics?* foodfacts.org fact-check (rated Misleading) — https://www.foodfacts.org/articles/can-fibre-help-your-body-excrete-microplastics

### "Probiotics / sealing 'leaky gut' blocks microplastic absorption" — `weak`
Causation is partly reversed (microplastics may *cause* gut leakiness); no human trials show probiotics block uptake. Preclinical only. Support gut health for general reasons; treat "seal your gut" product claims as unproven.
- *Microplastics and probiotics: mechanisms and health consequences* (review, 2025) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207257/

## (h) Policy / industry

### EU REACH restriction on intentionally-added microplastics (2023/2055) — `strong`
Binding EU law: bans loose plastic glitter and microbeads from 17 Oct 2023, with phased cosmetics deadlines to 2035; ECHA/Commission project preventing ~500,000 tonnes of microplastic release over 20 years (a projected impact, not measured). Targets deliberately-added particles, not chemicals or larger flows (tyre wear, textiles).
- Regulation (EU) 2023/2055, EUR-Lex — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2055/oj/eng
- ECHA, *World's broadest restriction of intentional uses of microplastics* — https://echa.europa.eu/en/-/working-on-the-world-s-broadest-restriction-of-intentional-uses-of-microplastics

### Global plastics treaty failed to reach consensus at INC-5.2 (Geneva, Aug 2025) — `strong`
UN talks under UNEA 5/14 adjourned 15 Aug 2025 without agreed text, deadlocked over production caps and chemicals of concern (High Ambition Coalition vs oil-producing "Like-Minded" bloc). No agreed text yet; future session expected.
- *Talks on global plastic pollution treaty adjourn without consensus*, UNEP (2025) — https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/talks-global-plastic-pollution-treaty-adjourn-without-consensus
- *Deadlock at INC-5.2*, Cambridge Prisms: Plastics (2025) — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-prisms-plastics/article/deadlock-at-inc52-understanding-the-blocked-progress-of-the-global-plastics-treaty-negotiations/2BFA16E1256326C731762F2B9810081F

### EU Single-Use Plastics Directive (2019/904) — `weak`
Binding; bans worst single-use items (cutlery, plates, straws, cotton-bud sticks) + EPR. A single 2023 modelling/citizen-science study suggests 13–40% litter reduction for targeted items depending on implementation (as low as 2–6% if only bans enforced). Doesn't address microplastics directly.
- Directive (EU) 2019/904, EUR-Lex — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/904/oj/eng
- Hinzmann, Kiessling et al., Waste Management (2023) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0956053X23002738

### Cosmetic microbead bans (US 2015, UK 2017/18) were narrow, never comprehensive — `strong`
Real and enforced, but cover only rinse-off products; microplastics remain in leave-on cosmetics, and microbeads were always ~2% of primary microplastics vs tyre wear/textiles. The EU's broader 2023/2055 was needed precisely because these were narrow.
- *Microbead-Free Waters Act 2015: FAQs*, FDA — https://www.fda.gov/drugs/laws-acts-and-rules/microbead-free-waters-act-2015-faqs
- IUCN, *Invisible plastic particles from textiles and tyres* (cosmetics ~2%) — https://iucn.org/news/secretariat/201702/invisible-plastic-particles-textiles-and-tyres-major-source-ocean-pollution-%E2%80%93-iucn-study

### Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR): raises recycling, not reduction — `emerging`
EPR reliably boosts collection/recycling but, alone, has not cut total plastic packaging produced (EU recycling rose while per-capita packaging waste kept rising). Needs eco-modulated fees + absolute reduction targets.
- OECD Environment Working Paper No. 225, *New Aspects of EPR* (2023) — https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/11/new-aspects-of-epr-extending-producer-responsibility-to-additional-product-groups-and-challenges-throughout-the-product-lifecycle_84483c40/cfdc1bdc-en.pdf
- Eurostat, *Plastic packaging waste in the EU* (2025) — https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20251022-1

### Popular actions EXCLUDED from the shortlist (and why)
- **Blood/plasma donation** — `unproven`: only PFAS-chemical evidence exists; no particle-removal data. Category error.
- **Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE)** — `weak`/commercial: single uncontrolled industry-funded study, proprietary unvalidated assay, no health outcomes, ~$5–8k/session, tubing can add particles.
- **Sauna / sweating** — `debunked`: particles too large for sweat glands; sweat may push plastic *additives into* skin.
- **Antioxidant / glutathione / NAC / CoQ10 "detox" supplements** — `unproven`: no human trials; nothing removes particles.
- **Fibre/raw-veg "flush"** — `weak`: no human evidence for these foods; the viral "57% reduction" figure is fabricated. (Eat fibre for other reasons.)
- **Probiotics to "seal leaky gut"** — `weak`: preclinical only; causation likely reversed.
- **HEPA air purifiers (marketed for microplastics)** — `weak`: cut general PM2.5 but microplastic-specific removal is manufacturer-claimed and partly contradicted. (A sealed HEPA *vacuum* is better supported than a purifier.)

## KEY NUMBERS WE CAN CITE WITH CONFIDENCE
- **~240,000 plastic particles/L** average in bottled water (range 110,000–370,000), ~90% nanoplastics — PNAS 2024. Frame as a *detection advance* (10–100× older estimates), not rising contamination.
- **Bottled water ≈ 3× more nanoplastic than treated tap** (same-method comparison) — Ohio State 2024.
- **~74,000–121,000 particles/year** most-cited human intake estimate (with inhalation); **bottled ~90,000/yr vs tap ~4,000/yr** — Cox et al. 2019. Always pair with "particle counts, wide uncertainty, only ~15% of diet sampled."
- **NEJM carotid-plaque study: HR 4.53 (~4.5×); polyethylene in 58.4% of plaques; n=304/257, ~34 mo** — Marfella et al. 2024. Always label "association, not causation; unreplicated."
- **EFSA cut the BPA TDI ~20,000× to 0.2 ng/kg bw/day (2023); EU BPA food-contact ban from 20 Jan 2025** — regulatory facts.
- **PFOA = IARC Group 1; PFOS = Group 2B (2023); US EPA limit 4 ppt for PFOA & PFOS (2024)** — regulatory facts (Group 1 = hazard, not measured risk at typical doses).
- **Dietary intervention: fresh food cut urinary BPA ~66%, DEHP ~53–56% in 3 days; canned soup raised urinary BPA ~1,221%** — Rudel/Carwile 2011.
- **EU REACH microplastics restriction: glitter/microbeads banned 17 Oct 2023; ~500,000 t prevented over 20 yrs (projected)**; **global treaty failed at INC-5.2, Aug 2025** — policy facts.

## NUMBERS TO AVOID CITING (overstated / debunked / artifact)
- **"5 g (a credit card) of plastic per week"** — `debunked`; extreme upper bound conflated with an inhalation study; realistic ≈ micrograms/week.
- **"~7 g / a spoonful of plastic in the brain" and the ~5,000 µg/g brain figure** — contested; likely lipid→polyethylene false positives; "very preliminary" per authors.
- **"11.6 billion + 3.1 billion microplastic particles per tea cup"** — 2–3 orders of magnitude too high (BfR 2025; mostly oligomer artifacts).
- **"356,238 / hundreds of thousands of CV deaths per year from phthalates"** and **"127 million metabolic-disease cases from bisphenols"** — causality-assuming attributable-burden models, not measured death tolls.
- **"Fibre cuts urinary microplastic biomarkers 57% (UCLA / EHP)"** — fabricated citation; do not use.
- **"WWTPs remove well over 95% of microplastics"** — overstated; realistic central tendency ~88–94% (only best-in-class MBRs reach >99%).
- **"Microwaving is uniquely dangerous"** — the driver is high temperature, not the microwave method per se (contested); and the ~4.2M/2.1B figures are per cm² lab simulant, not daily intake.
- **Black-utensil "near the EPA safety limit"** — withdrawn after two corrections; corrected exposure is <1% of the reference dose (the chemical *presence* stands).
- **Indoor "microplastic" fibre counts (0.4–59/m³)** — overstates the plastic share; most indoor airborne fibres are natural cellulosics.
